A FAILED DEBIT STOPS THE SIP BEFORE ANYONE CHOOSES TO LEAVE

1. What is the problem?

The metric is simple: of people who start a mutual-fund SIP, how many still have one running 12 months later? Two problems break that, and neither is “they chose to leave.”

Involuntary leak

A debit fails, the mandate expires, the bank changes, or the balance is short. The person did not decide to stop. In our tagged comments this is the largest in-scope group: 333 of 837 (39.8%). Only 0.9% of those 333 say the SIP actually ended.

Lack of knowledge

People ask how to start a SIP, which step is left, or why a debit failed. That shows up on setup (A2, 74 in-scope comments; top line is “how to start a SIP”) and in a pasted interview: the person did not know how to read the data, then withdrew and cancelled.

Comment counts: data/problem_candidates.csv and data/final_retag.csv, confident in-scope rows. Public comments, not customer rates. Groww itself has 17 C2 comments — under 30, so not a Groww rate.

Why this should be addressed Why it becomes a stop

  • What people say on a failed debit: “auto payment not working”, “repeated SIP failure”, “payment failed but money debited”. The step they take outside the app is Google.
  • AMFI, quoting SEBI: if 3 monthly instalments in a row fail, that SIP is treated as discontinued. A debit the app does not explain can be counted as a stop.
  • NPCI NACH, September 2026, computed by Measure of India: 25.2% of recurring debits came back unpaid (4.9 crore of 19.6 crore). This is EMI, SIP, insurance and bills together, not SIPs alone. Many are paid on a later try.
