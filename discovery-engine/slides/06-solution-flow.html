SOLUTIONING AND USER FLOW

MUST-HAVE FEATURES

1. Name the break (Decide)

How it works. The failed instalment opens on ₹2,000, due on the 5th, bank ending 4821. A chip, or one sentence, matches a screen. That match does not call the model.

Impact. 328 of 333 C2 comments (98.5%) are an instalment of a SIP already set up. The old page used one dead end for a rejected debit, a bank change, and money that had already left.

2. One safe step (Act)

How it works. A rejected debit and an autopay that did not send both offer “Pay this instalment,” and only this ₹2,000. Money already left has no pay button. A bank change opens the form: the account-holder name is optional, and bank, account, phone, and address stay ***.

Impact. Kritika, 30, had no form and no email when her bank changed. Madhuri, 26, had money cut that was not in the NAV. A pay button on her screen would charge the month again.

3. Close this month (Finish)

How it works. Debit path: month received. Bank path: masked details saved. Money-left path: units by 7 November on the demo, or ₹2,000 back. Amount stays ₹2,000. Next date stays 5 December. Confirming does not move money.

Impact. 289 of 333 (86.8%) say the SIP is still continuing. 3 (0.9%) say it ended. A fix in time, C3, is 4 of 837 (0.5%). The month is where these people still are.

4. Helper, then a person (Ask)

How it works. The chat opens with “Hi, what can I help you with?” A matched break offers “Yes, do it.” Not now leaves the screen as it is. Any other question uses NVIDIA, temperature 0, at most two calls. Yes ends it. “No, connect me with support” points to Help in the Groww app. This demo cannot place a call. A handoff from the notes is a demo ticket nobody receives. History lasts this visit.

Impact. Manas, 23, wanted a bot on the open page and a way to support. Madhuri’s in-app AI and support both stopped short. The helper tries first.

LATER, NOT THIS FLOW

  • Could. A live debit and allotment with the bank and the fund house.
